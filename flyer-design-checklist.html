Flyer Design Checklist: What Every Promotional Flyer Needs

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

A flyer has about two seconds to earn a second look. Whether it is taped to a coffee shop board in Benson or posted to your Instagram Story, the same rules apply: one message, the key details, and one clear next step. Use this checklist before you print or post.

1. One headline that says why to care

Lead with the benefit or the event, not your business name. "$5 Off Every Cut, Tuesdays" works better than "Fresh Fade Barbershop Presents." Keep it to about six words and make it the largest text on the page.

2. The details people need to act

  • What it is (event, sale, grand opening, new service)
  • When: day, date and time. Write out the day: "Saturday, Dec 12."
  • Where: full address, or "online" with the link
  • How much: price, discount or "free"
  • Any limits: "while supplies last," "first 50 guests," expiration date

If someone has to message you to ask one of these, the flyer is missing it.

3. One clear call to action

Tell people exactly what to do: call, scan, book, DM, show this flyer at checkout. One action works better than five.

4. Your contact info and branding

Logo, business name, and one or two ways to reach you. Use your real brand colors so the flyer matches your sign and social media.

5. A clean visual hierarchy

Readers should see things in this order: headline, image, key details, call to action, contact. Use size and color to create that order. If everything is bold, nothing is.

6. Readable fonts and contrast

Stick to one or two fonts. Avoid thin or script fonts for important details. Check that text is readable from across a room. Light text needs a dark background behind it, and the reverse.

7. Real images (or no images)

A sharp photo of your actual product, space or team beats generic stock photos. If you don't have a good photo, a bold text-only design can work better than a blurry picture.

8. The right sizes for print and social

Use Size
Standard print flyer 8.5 × 11 in (letter)
Half-page handout 5.5 × 8.5 in
Instagram or Facebook feed 1080 × 1350 px (4:5) or 1080 × 1080 px
Instagram or Facebook Story 1080 × 1920 px (9:16)

Designing once and resizing for each spot saves time and keeps everything consistent.

9. The right files

  • Print: a high-resolution PDF. Ask your printer whether they need a bleed (extra image past the trim edge).
  • Social: a PNG or JPG at the pixel sizes above.

10. Proofread twice

Wrong dates are the most common and most expensive flyer mistake. Have someone else read every date, time, price and address before you print.

Bonus: bilingual flyers

If your customers speak Spanish, an English/Spanish flyer can reach more people. Keep both languages the same size and layout so neither looks like an afterthought.
