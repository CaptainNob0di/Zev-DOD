DIY in Canva vs. Hiring a Designer: When Each Makes Sense

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Canva has made it possible for anyone to make a decent-looking flyer in an afternoon. So do small businesses still need a designer? Sometimes yes, sometimes no. Here's an honest way to decide, from a designer who also uses Canva.

When DIY in Canva makes sense

  • You post a lot of quick, everyday content. "Open today," "sold out," and weather updates don't need a designer.
  • You already have brand templates. If your colors, fonts and layouts are set, swapping text and photos is fast.
  • Your budget is very tight and your time is cheaper than a designer's fee right now.
  • You enjoy it. Some owners like designing, and it shows.

When hiring a designer makes more sense

  • It's your logo or brand. Canva's license terms limit how its stock elements can be used in logos and trademarks, so a logo built on template graphics may not be fully yours to register. Check Canva's current license terms before building a logo there.
  • It's printed in quantity. Menus, signs and large flyer runs are expensive to reprint if the file isn't print-ready.
  • It needs to stand out. Popular templates show up everywhere. Customers may have seen yours on another business's post.
  • Time matters more than money. If a flyer takes you three hours and a designer charges about what one customer spends, the math often favors hiring.
  • You keep putting it off. A flyer that never gets made costs more than any design fee.

A side-by-side look

DIY in Canva Hiring a designer
Cost Free or a monthly subscription Per project
Your time Hours per design A short brief and feedback
Uniqueness Templates are widely shared Built for your business
Print-ready files Possible, easy to get wrong Handled for you
Logo ownership Limited by template and stock licenses Original work you can own
Consistency Depends on you Built into the system

The best of both: hire once, DIY after

Many small businesses do best with a mix:

  1. Hire a designer for the foundation: your logo, colors, fonts and a few key pieces like a menu or main flyer.
  2. Ask for editable Canva templates built on that foundation.
  3. DIY the everyday posts by swapping text and photos in those templates.

You get a professional, consistent look without paying for every Story.

Tips if you DIY

  • Use your real brand colors (save them as a Brand Kit) instead of each template's colors.
  • Limit yourself to two fonts.
  • Replace stock photos with your own whenever possible.
  • Export print files as PDF Print and check the size before sending to a printer.
  • Proofread dates, prices and addresses twice.
