How Often Should a Small Business Post on Facebook and Instagram?

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

There's no magic number, and anyone who promises one is guessing. What matters most is a schedule you can keep up for months, with posts good enough that people stop scrolling. Here's a practical way to decide.

Consistency beats volume

Posting every day for two weeks and then disappearing for a month hurts more than it helps. Customers who check your page see a business that might be closed. A steady rhythm, even a modest one, tells people you're open, active and reliable.

Practical starting points

Your situation Feed posts Stories Short videos
Just getting started, very busy 2 per week When something happens Optional
Social brings in bookings or foot traffic 3 per week 3+ per week 2 per month
Social is a main sales channel 4–5 per week Most days 1 per week or more

Pick the row you can sustain, not the one you wish you could do. You can always move up.

Facebook vs. Instagram

For most local businesses, Facebook and Instagram can share the same core content, especially if you schedule through Meta Business Suite. A few adjustments help:

  • Instagram rewards strong visuals, Reels and Stories. Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, including a local one.
  • Facebook is often where older customers and community groups are. Event details, hours and offers do well. Hashtags matter less.
  • Google Business Profile is often overlooked. Posting updates and offers there can help people who search for you on Google and Maps.

What counts as a "post"?

Not every post needs a new photo shoot. Rotate formats so the work stays manageable:

  • A single photo or graphic
  • A carousel (several images people swipe through)
  • A short video or Reel
  • A Story: quick, casual, and gone after 24 hours

Stories are the easiest place to post more often. They're good for "open today," quick polls and behind-the-scenes moments.

Best time to post?

The best time is when your customers are on their phones. Check your own numbers: Instagram's professional dashboard and Meta Business Suite insights show when your followers are most active. A restaurant might post before lunch and dinner rushes. A home-services business might do better in the evening when homeowners are planning.

Make a schedule you can keep

  1. Choose your posting days and write them down.
  2. Batch your content once a month: plan, write and design in one sitting.
  3. Schedule everything in advance with Meta Business Suite (free) or another scheduler.
  4. Leave room for real-time posts about weather closures, sold-out items and new arrivals.
  5. Review your insights once a month and adjust.

Signs you need help

  • Your last post is more than two weeks old
  • You only post when business is slow
  • Your posts don't look like they come from the same business
  • You spend Sunday nights stressing about content
