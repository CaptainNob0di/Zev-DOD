How to Set Up Google Business Profile for Your Omaha Small Business

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

When someone in Omaha searches "barbershop near me" or "food truck Benson," Google often shows a map with a few businesses before any websites. Those listings come from Google Business Profile, a free tool from Google. If you serve local customers and haven't set it up, it's one of the highest-value hours you can spend on marketing.

Before you start

Have these ready:

  • Your exact business name as customers see it (no extra keywords stuffed in, which is against Google's guidelines)
  • Address, or the areas you serve if you go to customers
  • Phone number and website
  • Hours, including holiday hours
  • 5 to 10 good photos: storefront, interior, work, products, team

Step 1: Create or claim your profile

  1. Go to google.com/business and sign in with a Google account you'll keep long-term. Use a business account, not a personal one you might lose access to.
  2. Search for your business name. If it already exists, claim it. If not, create it.
  3. Choose your primary category carefully. It's one of the most important settings. Pick the one that best describes your core business ("Barber shop," "Soul food restaurant," "Pressure washing service"). You can add secondary categories later.

Step 2: Storefront or service area?

  • Customers visit you (shop, restaurant, studio): add your address.
  • You go to customers (cleaning, lawn care, mobile services): hide your address and list your service areas, for example Omaha, Bellevue, Papillion, La Vista, Ralston, Elkhorn and Council Bluffs.

Step 3: Verify

Google needs to confirm you're the real business. Depending on your business, it may offer a video recording, a phone or text code, email, or a postcard. Follow the steps Google shows you. Your profile won't appear fully until it's verified.

Step 4: Fill out everything

Complete profiles give customers fewer reasons to choose someone else:

  • Description: what you do, who you serve and what makes you different, in plain language
  • Hours: regular, plus special hours for holidays and events
  • Services or menu: with prices if you're comfortable sharing them
  • Attributes: such as wheelchair accessible, Black-owned, women-owned, or Spanish spoken, only if true
  • Booking or ordering link, if you have one

Step 5: Add photos regularly

Upload real photos, not stock images. Add new ones every few weeks: new menu items, finished jobs, seasonal displays. Profiles with current photos look open and active.

Step 6: Post updates and offers

Google Business Profile has its own posts: updates, offers and events. Reuse your social media content here. A weekly post about a special, event or new arrival keeps your profile fresh.

Step 7: Ask for and answer reviews

  • Ask happy customers for a review. A short link or QR code at checkout makes it easy.
  • Never buy reviews, offer payment for them, or write fake ones. It breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad, politely and quickly. Future customers read your replies.

Step 8: Keep your info consistent everywhere

Make sure your name, address, phone and hours match on your website, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp and anywhere else you're listed. Inconsistent details can confuse both customers and search engines.

A simple monthly routine (20 minutes)

  • Post one update or offer
  • Add 2 to 3 new photos
  • Reply to new reviews and questions
  • Check hours for upcoming holidays
