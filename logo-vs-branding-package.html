Logo or Full Brand? What a Small-Business Branding Package Includes

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

A lot of new business owners ask for "a logo" when what they really need is a small set of tools that make everything they publish look like it came from the same place. That set is your brand identity, and it doesn't have to be expensive or complicated.

Logo vs. brand identity

A logo is the mark itself: your business name in a specific style, sometimes with a symbol.

A brand identity is the system around it:

  • The logo in a few versions (full color, black, white, small icon)
  • A color palette, usually 3 to 5 colors with exact codes
  • A font pairing, one for headlines and one for body text
  • A simple brand sheet showing how they all fit together

With only a logo, every flyer, menu and post starts from scratch, and things slowly drift apart. With a basic identity, anyone can make something that looks on-brand.

Nice extras include a social media profile picture version, a favicon for your website, and editable templates.

File types, explained simply

  • PNG with a transparent background: for social media, websites and putting your logo on photos.
  • PDF: for printers.
  • Vector files (SVG, EPS or AI): best for large signs, vehicle wraps and embroidery, because they scale to any size without getting blurry. Ask whether these are included.

Make sure you can actually own it

If you plan to trademark your logo, it should be built from original shapes and properly licensed fonts, not stock icons or template graphics that thousands of other businesses can use. Ask your designer:

  • Is this logo built from original artwork?
  • Are the fonts licensed for commercial use?
  • Do I own the final design once I've paid?

Trademark rules have details that matter, so talk to a trademark attorney or check the USPTO's guidance before you file.

Signs you're ready for branding (not just a logo)

  • You're about to order signs, shirts or printed menus
  • Your social posts all look different from each other
  • You're opening a location or relaunching
  • You're paying for ads and want people to recognize you

How to get the most out of a branding project

  1. Bring examples of brands you like, and ones you don't.
  2. Describe your customers: who they are and what they care about.
  3. Pick three words for how you want to feel to customers (bold, warm, trusted...).
  4. Give feedback as one combined list per round, so revisions stay focused.
