How Much Does Social Media Management Cost for an Omaha Small Business?

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 4 min read

The honest answer is that social media management can cost anywhere from a little more than a phone bill to more than a full-time employee. The price depends almost entirely on how much work is being done each month, not on who is doing it. Once you know what drives that work, you can compare quotes in Omaha and see whether a price is fair.

What you are actually paying for

Every social media package is a bundle of a few jobs. When you get a quote, ask how many of these are included and how much of each:

  1. Planning. Deciding what to post, when, and why. Usually a monthly content calendar.
  2. Writing. Captions, hashtags, calls to action and alt text for each post.
  3. Design. Graphics, carousels and Story frames in your brand colors.
  4. Video. Editing short Reels or TikToks from clips you film.
  5. Scheduling. Loading posts into Meta Business Suite or a scheduler so they go out on time.
  6. Community management. Checking comments and messages and replying or flagging them.
  7. Reporting. Pulling numbers from the platforms and explaining what they mean.
  8. Paid ads. Managing ad budgets and targeting. This is a separate skill and is usually priced separately, plus the ad spend itself.

A low price usually means fewer posts, fewer platforms and little or no video or community management. A high price usually adds ads, daily engagement, on-site photo shoots or strategy meetings.

The four things that move the price most

Number of posts per month. Eight posts a month (two a week) is a very different workload from twenty. Each post needs an idea, a caption, a graphic and a final check.

Number of platforms. Facebook and Instagram can often share content through Meta Business Suite. Adding Google Business Profile or TikTok adds formatting and posting time.

Video. Editing a 20-second Reel well takes longer than designing a graphic. Packages that include Reels cost more for a good reason.

Who answers your messages. If someone is checking comments and DMs several times a week, that time has to be paid for.

Questions to ask before you sign anything

  • How many posts per month, on which platforms?
  • Do I approve posts before they go live?
  • Who owns the graphics and captions if I cancel?
  • Is it month-to-month, or is there a contract? How much notice to cancel?
  • Do you need my password? (The answer should be no. See our guide to giving a social media manager safe access.)
  • What is not included?

Be careful with anyone who guarantees a number of followers or sales. Nobody controls the algorithm, and guaranteed follower growth is often a sign of bought followers, which can hurt your account.

A simple way to pick a budget

Start from what you need to stay visible, not from the biggest package.

  • You just need to stop going quiet: about two posts a week on one platform is enough to show customers you are open and active.
  • You rely on Instagram and Facebook for bookings or foot traffic: three posts a week on both, plus a couple of short videos a month, is a solid baseline.
  • Social is a main sales channel: near-daily posting, regular Reels, and someone watching your messages.

If you are unsure, start smaller for two or three months, look at what is working, then upgrade. A good provider will tell you when not to upgrade.
