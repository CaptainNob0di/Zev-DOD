How to Give a Social Media Manager Access Without Sharing Your Password

By Isaiah Lewis, Zev DOD · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

If a social media manager asks for your Facebook or Instagram password, that's a red flag. You never need to share it. Meta, the company behind Facebook and Instagram, has built-in ways to give someone access to your business accounts and take it back anytime. Here's how it works and why it matters.

Why you shouldn't share passwords

  • You can lose control of your account. Whoever has the password can change it, change the recovery email, or lock you out.
  • It can trigger security checks. Logins from new devices and locations can lead to verification checks or temporary locks.
  • You can't control what they can do. Password access is all-or-nothing, including billing and deleting the page.
  • Removing access is messy. You have to change your password and hope nothing else was changed.

Partner or team access fixes all of this.

The safe way: Meta Business Suite partner access

If your Facebook Page and Instagram account are connected to a Meta business portfolio (formerly Business Manager), you can add an agency or freelancer as a partner:

  1. Open Meta Business Suite settings at business.facebook.com/settings, on a computer or in the app.
  2. Go to Users → Partners.
  3. Choose to add a partner and give them access to your assets.
  4. Enter the partner's business portfolio ID. Your manager will send you this number.
  5. Select your Facebook Page and Instagram account.
  6. Choose permissions: usually content, messages and insights. Leave ad accounts and billing off unless you've agreed they'll run ads.
  7. Save, and let your manager know.

Meta updates these menus from time to time, so labels may differ slightly. Look for "Partners" or "Business assets" in your settings.

No business portfolio? Other options

  • Facebook Page access: in your Page settings, under Page access, you can invite someone and choose whether they get full or partial control.
  • Instagram: a professional Instagram account connected to your Facebook Page can be managed through Meta Business Suite once the Page access is set up.
  • Schedulers like Buffer: you connect the account yourself by logging in on your own device. The scheduler stores a secure connection, not your password.

Removing access

When you stop working with someone, go back to Users → Partners (or Page access), remove them, and confirm. It takes effect right away. A good manager will remove themselves and confirm in writing when you cancel.

A few more security habits

  • Turn on two-factor authentication on your Facebook and Instagram accounts.
  • Keep at least two trusted admins (for example you and a business partner) so you're never locked out.
  • Review access every few months and remove anyone who no longer needs it.
  • Be suspicious of messages claiming your page will be deleted unless you click a link. Those are common scams.
